## Phase 0: the baseline in codemode-chat

Both briefs as text tasks on the work board, three agents (one per model), the forum's seven criteria, two
improvement rounds, budget $4 each.

| Brief | Time | Work items | Tokens | Cost | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Website | 1.8 min | 37 | 85 261 | $0.21 | 7 answers, none meets all 7 criteria (best: 4 of 7) |
| Resolution | 1.9 min | 42 | 85 564 | $0.22 | 8 answers, none meets all 7 criteria (best: 5 of 7) |

Where it breaks:

- A text task's question holds 2000 characters and no document: the requirements (3.4 kB) had to be cut down to
  keywords inside the question.
- No sources: the resolution brief ran without the repository. The answers say so themselves ("Real paths and
  quotes still need repo access", "assumes that the world uses a voxel grid").
- An answer is one model call of at most about 1250 output tokens: a page, not a plan document.
- There is no common document: the run leaves seven or eight competing answers and, here, none as its result.

The results are in `docs/live/results/website-1.md` to `website-3.md`, each with its generated appendix. A run with

Against the plan's estimate (200 to 250 calls a run): the measured runs stayed under it and cost $3.9 to $4.8.

### What the runs showed, and what was changed

1. **A path check without a repository (run 1).** Table A ended after one round because the checker found
   `view.ts` "not in the sources": the task had an uploaded document only, so every file the plan names is new.
   The path check now reports a missing path only when a repository is among the sources (`server/checks.ts`).
2. **One reviser against two objectors (run 1).** At table B the same section was handed back unchanged three
   times although two models objected to it; the objections stood as declined and no majority was possible. A
   reviser can no longer leave a section as it is when more than half of the other models object to it; the prompt
   says so (`server/run.ts`, `server/calls.ts`).
3. **Evidence that was there and did not count (run 2).** In round 3 at table A all three models approved and no
   approval counted. Sonnet and GPT had quoted the plan, but several quotes in one line (`"…" and "…"`) or a quote
   with a remark of their own, and the whole line had to stand in the plan. Now one stretch of at least three
   words between quotation marks or ellipses is enough (`server/ballots.ts`). Replayed against the ballots of run
   2, both approvals would have counted.
4. The closing line of a final round said "0 of 3 models answered the roll call" (it has none); corrected.

### What stays open

- **No agreement in three rounds.** In run 3, with all fixes, GPT and Sonnet rejected in every round at both
  tables; each round raised new objections (27 to 36 a table and run). Whether more rounds, or the rule against
  moving targets from an earlier round on, lead to an agreement is not measured.
- **Codestral's approvals never counted.** It approved in almost every round, mostly quoting the criteria
  themselves or text that is not in the plan. The check does what it is for; with three models it means the
  majority has to come from the other two.
- **A provider that fails in the middle of a run.** When the Anthropic account ran out, the run went on with
  notes ("did not reply") and ended; the next run was refused at the roll call for $0.0007. Nothing was lost,
  but a run does not say up front that a model's account is the cause.
- The repository brief (reading in steps in a repository of 2214 files) has not run with real models.

## The results page

`tests/live/collect.ts` takes everything a run left out of the space (run it against a stack started with
`FORUM_LLM=0` on the runs' store), and `tests/live/report.ts` turns the collected runs into one static page with a
data file per run (`--notes docs/live/notes.de.json` adds the German text around them). The page shows each run step
by step: the kickoff, the outlines and the pick, the first draft and its check, every round of each table with its
objections, the difference each revision made, the checks and every ballot with each criterion and why it did or
did not count, the blind comparison, the final round, the result, every version of both documents with the
difference between any two, the discussion with its decisions, the cost by model and kind of call, and the room's
lines. It is made anew after every run.

## The concept for the remaining runs (revised 2026-11-23)

What is negotiated in a run: each model's understanding of the brief and its questions; the outline (at table B
every model proposes one and picks another's); the text of every section, each with its author; objections (a
section, a concrete defect, what would resolve it), which the reviser mends or leaves and the raiser confirms;
proposals in the discussion, which become decisions by majority; and after every round one ballot per model that
rates the seven criteria with quotes from the plan.

Changes proposed before the next runs (not made yet; they wait for the person's word):

- Five rounds instead of three, as the plan's default. What a run must show: whether it ends agreed, in which round,
  and whether the objections a round raises go down from round to round.
- Small uploaded documents (up to about 16 kB) go into every prompt whole, instead of every model reading them first;
  a path with a leading `/` or `docs/` is taken for the uploaded file. (Some early drafts of run 3 invented
  requirements after a read under a wrong path; the results of all three runs name R1 to R24 correctly.)
- One short call per model before a run; a run in which a model failed is marked as not comparable (run 3: Sonnet
  failed from round 3 of table A on, when the Anthropic account ran out).
- The brief may be given in German, which makes the plans and the appendix German.
- The requirements of the website brief are invented for these runs (a joinery with 24 requirements, tight on
  budget and time, with conflicts on purpose); the person may give a list of their own instead.
